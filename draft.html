Dāvis Zavickis

I am a research assistant and PhD candidate in physics at the University of Latvia, developing first-principles electronic-structure methods for “exciting,” a full-potential all-electron density-functional-theory code based on linearised augmented plane waves.

Previously, I worked as an engineer at the Institute of Solid State Physics, University of Latvia, using kinetic Monte Carlo and density-functional theory methods to study condensed-matter systems and predict new materials for intermediate-temperature ceramic fuel cells. In parallel, I worked as a data scientist at the Latvian Environment, Geology and Meteorology Centre, building post-processing systems for deterministic and ensemble weather forecasts.

I received my BSc and MSc in physics, both with distinction, from the University of Latvia, including an exchange semester at Umeå University, Sweden. My research interests include density-functional theory, electronic-structure methods, high-performance scientific computing, and statistical methods for environmental forecasting.
